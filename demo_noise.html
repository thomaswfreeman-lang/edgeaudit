Adjusted R is the slice average pulled toward your overall average by an amount set by how much of the spread between slices is explainable as noise. When the spread between your slices is entirely noise, every adjusted figure collapses onto your overall average — which is the correct answer to "which setup is my best", and the one most tools will not give you.

Method

  • Intervals from a stationary block bootstrap (4,000 resamples), so streaks and tilt-clusters are not mistaken for independent observations.
  • Each slice tested twice: against zero, and against your own overall average by label permutation. A slice that only beats zero is inheriting your global edge, not adding one.
  • Multiplicity controlled with Benjamini-Hochberg across all 32 tested slices at q=0.10. Slices below the minimum sample were excluded from the family, not quietly counted.
  • Slice averages shrunk toward the global mean by empirical Bayes.
  • Sharpe figures reported as Probabilistic and Deflated Sharpe (Bailey & López de Prado), adjusting for skew, kurtosis, sample length and the number of slices searched.
  • Serial dependence check: lag-1 autocorrelation -0.038, runs-test z +0.51 — no material dependence detected.
  • Session bands assume exchange local time (US futures). Timezone-free exports are read as supplied.
